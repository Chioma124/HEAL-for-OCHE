Our Mission

The Health and Education Access Hub aims to address the persistent barriers Children with Special Health Care Needs face in accessing appropriate education. Our long-term goal is to prevent any child's education from being compromised due to health and to promote equity for children and families navigating both the healthcare and education systems.

Why This Matters

Every year, thousands of children with serious health conditions spend extended periods of time in the hospital. Although laws protect the educational rights of these children, many families face significant challenges when their child returns to school or needs to access special education services. Communication between hospitals and schools is often poor, resources are limited, and families — especially those who speak languages other than English — struggle to navigate a complicated system.

We are collaborating with Parents Helping Parents (PHP), a respected parent training and information center based in San Jose, to build coordinated, comprehensive solutions for children with special health care needs.

About the HEAL Program

Hospital Educational Advocacy Liaisons (HEAL) helps children succeed in school after they are diagnosed with a medical condition or disability. It is a free program that works with you to find support that meets your child's learning and behavioral needs.

HEAL serves children who:

  • Are patients of Stanford Medicine Children's Health
  • Are 3 to 18 years old
  • Have medical conditions or disabilities that affect learning

Guardian Advocacy

Resources to help guardians understand and advocate for their children's rights in the school system. Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), your child has the right to a free appropriate public education in the least restrictive environment. The resources below help you understand and exercise those rights.

  • Special Education and Your Rights — How to get services, contest evaluation results, ensure your child's Individualized Education Plan (IEP) is being followed, and what to do if your child is suspended or removed from school.
    English · Spanish · PDF
  • 2022 Procedural Safeguards — Overview of the legal rights and procedural safeguards guaranteed to students with disabilities and their guardians under IDEA. Schools must provide this to you at least once per year.
    English · PDF
  • Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) — If you disagree with the school's evaluation of your child, you have the right to request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) conducted by a qualified professional outside the school district — at no cost to you.
    English · PDF
  • Special Ed Advocacy Tips (DRC 2020) — Practical tips for guardians before, during, and after an IEP meeting — including how to push back when denied services and how to document communications with the school district.
    English · Spanish · PDF

IEP Resources

An Individualized Education Plan (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines your child's educational needs, goals, and the services the school will provide. Children from preschool through 12th grade may qualify. Once you submit an evaluation request, the school must respond within 15 days and complete the assessment within 60 days.

  • 18 Tips for IEP Meetings — How to approach an IEP meeting, what to say, how to conduct yourself, and how to ensure proper follow-up after the meeting. Includes tips on documenting what was agreed upon.
    English · Spanish · PDF
  • IEP and Me — Video Series — A series of short videos educating guardians and providers about the IEP process from start to finish. Covers eligibility, goal-setting, placement, and family rights.
    English · Video
  • Types of Special Education and How to Apply — Step-by-step guide explaining special education, how to request an evaluation, what happens after you submit a consent form, and what an IEP covers. Includes key timelines and what to do if the school says no.
    English · PDF
  • What is a 504 Plan? — If your child does not qualify for special education under IDEA, they may qualify for a 504 plan under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act. A 504 plan can include accommodations like extra time on tests, testing in a quiet room, or preferential seating.
    English · PDF

Template Letters

  • Guardian Request: Special Education Assessment Letter — A letter for guardians to formally request that the school district conduct a special education assessment to determine whether their child is eligible for services.
    English · Spanish · Word Document
  • MD Request: Special Education Assessment Letter — A letter for medical providers to formally request a special education assessment on behalf of a patient. Schools are more likely to act promptly on requests that include medical documentation.
    English · Word Document
  • IEP Meeting Request Letter — Template for formally requesting an IEP meeting with your school district. You have the right to request an IEP meeting at any time — you do not have to wait for the annual review.
    English · Spanish · Word Document
  • Letter Requesting Evaluation Results — Template for requesting copies of your child's assessment results and reports from the school district. You have the right to receive these in a language you understand.
    English · Spanish · Word Document

    English · PDF

Special Services

Information about special education services, therapies, and support programs available through schools and the hospital. Under an IEP, schools can provide related services including speech-language pathology (SLP), occupational therapy (OT), physical therapy (PT), and applied behavior analysis (ABA) if these are necessary for your child to benefit from their education.

  • Assistive Technology — Myths and Facts — Guide clarifying common misconceptions about assistive technology (AT) devices in schools, including augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) devices and other communication tools. Assistive technology can be written into an IEP as a related service.
    English · PDF
  • Sample Accommodations for Anxious Children — A list of school accommodations appropriate for children with anxiety disorders that can be included in a 504 plan or IEP, including flexible attendance policies and reduced assignment loads.
    English · Word Document
  • Speech-Language IEP Accommodations — Accommodation guide for children with speech and language needs — what to ask for in an IEP and how to ensure speech-language pathology (SLP) services are provided consistently.
    English · PDF
  • Medical Educational Placements — Overview of educational placement options for children with medical conditions, including special day classes (SDC), home hospital instruction, independent study, and county office of education (SCOE) programs.
    English · PDF
  • Thrive Program — Cardiology — Timely mental health assessment and support for children in the Cardiology program at Stanford Medicine Children's Health.
    English · Stanford Medicine Children's Health
  • Ollie's Branch — Support program for siblings of children with complex medical needs at Stanford Medicine Children's Health.
    English · Stanford Medicine Children's Health

Community Resources

Community organizations and programs that can support families navigating the special education system. Regional centers such as SARC (Self Advocacy Resource Center) and GGRC (Golden Gate Regional Center) provide services to individuals with developmental disabilities, though the quality of service can vary significantly depending on your caseworker and county.

  • SARC — Self Advocacy Resource Center — Regional center offering self-determination programs and service coordination for individuals with developmental disabilities. Getting a good service coordinator makes a significant difference. Note: self-determination is a 2-year process — plan ahead.
    English · Spanish · Vietnamese
  • PHP / AbilityPath — Family Resource Centers — Community family resource centers offering support, connections, and advocacy help for families of children with disabilities in the Bay Area.
    English · Spanish
  • Brightlife / Soluna — Community mental health resources for children and families in the Bay Area, including counseling and support groups.
    English · Spanish
  • Child Mind Institute — Resources and support for children with mental health and learning disorders, including guides for parents and educators on ADHD, anxiety, learning disabilities, and more.
    English · External Link
  • Decoding Dyslexia — Parent-led organization supporting families of children with dyslexia and other reading-related learning disabilities. Includes state-specific chapters and advocacy resources.
    English · External Link
  • Linda Mood Bell / Children's Health Council / Charles Armstrong School — Alternative teaching and learning support programs in the Bay Area for children who need additional academic support beyond what the school district provides.
    English
